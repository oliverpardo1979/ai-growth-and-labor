\section{Numerical algorithm and equilibrium audit}
\label{app:rewrite-algorithm}

\subsection{Numerical solution}

For the RSI-activation comparisons in
Sections~\ref{subsec:rewrite-rsi-half-decline} and~\ref{subsec:rewrite-rsi-research-share},
I compute numerical approximations to equilibrium trajectories by solving
a boundary-value problem (BVP). Initial capital $K_0$ and AI efficiency
$B_0$ are given, whereas initial consumption $C_0$ and the developer's
shadow price $q_0$ are determined jointly with the transition.
I solve the equilibrium differential equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-equilibrium-dynamics} over a finite horizon,
using the given stocks as initial conditions and the long-run
characterization in Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-equilibrium-regimes}
to construct terminal conditions.

\phantomsection\label{app:rewrite-competitive-transition-numerics}
For each $\sigma$, I obtain the pre-RSI capital stock from
Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-rsi-initial-ratio} under monopoly pricing with
efficiency fixed at $B_0$. For the competitive-to-monopoly comparison in
Section~\ref{subsec:rewrite-competitive-transition}, I instead impose
$r=\rho+\gamma$ with marginal-cost pricing $p_X=1/B_0$, giving
$K_0=6.535$ at $\sigma=1.5$. In both cases, the resource constraint
with $M=0$ and $\dot K=(n+\gamma)K$ gives pre-event consumption.
The post-event monopoly BVP inherits the stocks but selects consumption,
research, and the shadow price anew.

In the normalized coordinates of Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-finite-existence},
I require the terminal deviation from the limiting point to lie in the
stable subspace of the linearized dynamics. The resulting two terminal
restrictions, together with the initial stocks, select initial consumption
and the shadow price without requiring the economy to reach its long-run
limit at a finite date.

I solve this BVP with \texttt{scipy.integrate.solve\_bvp} from the
open-source Python library SciPy \citep{virtanenetal2020}. The solver
implements fourth-order collocation with residual control similar to that
of \citet{kierzenkashampine2001}. At each trial state, I solve the static
monopoly problem and recover research expenditure from its first-order
condition.

\subsection{Accuracy and equilibrium checks}

I check the dynamic and first-order conditions independently of the
solver's residual report, tighten tolerances, and extend each horizon
twice. I also check pre-event conditions, continuity at RSI activation,
proximity to the limiting equilibrium, and the asymptotic decay of both
transversality expressions at rate $n-\rho<0$. The
\href{https://oliverpardo1979.github.io/ai-growth-and-labor/\#app:rewrite-numerical-details}{digital edition}
reports the computational coordinates, continuation procedure, tolerances,
and accuracy table. All plotted values lie within the solved interval.
The competitive-to-monopoly paths satisfy the same checks.

\phantomsection\label{app:rewrite-numerical-support-checks}
Small equation residuals do not establish global developer optimality.
All eight RSI-activation configurations have $\alpha=0.33$, $\eta=0.20$,
and $B_0/\overline B=0.01$. For each of the four elasticities
$\sigma\in\{0.90,1.00,1.10,1.50\}$,
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-optimality-condition} holds because
\[
 \eta\left(1-\frac{B_0}{\overline B}\right)
 =0.20(1-0.01)=0.198<0.33
 =\min\left\{\alpha,\frac1\sigma\right\}.
\]
This condition does not depend on the dated values of capital and
effective labor or on research productivity $\chi$.
The baseline competitive-to-monopoly comparisons use the same parameters
and initial efficiency ratio, so the same calculation applies.
The supplementary growth-reversal exercise instead has
$B_0/\overline B=0.90$, giving $0.20(1-0.90)=0.02<0.33$.

The long-run limits used to construct the terminal conditions imply a
finite present value of net profit for any feasible trajectory attaining
those limits. Let $D_t=\exp(-\int_0^t r_s\,\dd s)$.
Discounted output $D_tY_t$ has limiting logarithmic growth $n-\rho<0$,
and operating profit is bounded above by output. Research expenditure
converges to a finite level, while the interest rate converges to a
positive limit. Both discounted operating profit and discounted research
expenditure are therefore integrable.

Together with the parameter restriction verified above, these properties
allow Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification} to establish global
developer optimality for a feasible trajectory satisfying the research
condition, costate equation, and transversality condition.
The numerical checks assess how closely the computed paths satisfy these
conditions over the solved horizon and approach the required long-run
limits. They do not prove equilibrium existence from arbitrary initial
stocks.

\paragraph{Replication files.}

I use NumPy for array operations and linear algebra \citep{harrisetal2020}
and Matplotlib to generate the figures \citep{hunter2007}.
The code and simulation data are available in the
\href{https://github.com/oliverpardo1979/ai-growth-and-labor}{repository}.
The \href{https://github.com/oliverpardo1979/ai-growth-and-labor/blob/main/REPLICATION.md}
{replication guide} records package versions, implementation details,
numerical tolerances, and commands for checking and reproducing the
results, with links to the additional institutional experiments.
