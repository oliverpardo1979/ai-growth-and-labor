\subsection{Developer optimization}
\label{app:rewrite-developer-proofs}

The research problem uses operating profit after AI-service supply has
been chosen optimally. I first establish that
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} identifies the unique
profit-maximizing supply, so I can use this condition when constructing
equilibrium trajectories.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Optimal AI-service supply]
\label{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice}
The developer's operating profit in
problem~\eqref{eq:rewrite-service-choice} has a unique global maximizer
$X>0$. This optimal supply of AI services is characterized by
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} and varies smoothly with
$K,A,L,$ and $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice}]
Hold \(K,A,L,B\) fixed and let \(R(X)=p_X(X)X\) denote AI-service revenue.
I show that marginal revenue crosses the constant marginal cost \(1/B\)
exactly once, from above, so operating profit has a unique global maximum.
The inverse-demand elasticity in \eqref{eq:rewrite-inverse-demand-elasticity}
and differentiation of \eqref{eq:rewrite-sx} give
\[
 R'(X)=p_X(X)(1-e_X),\qquad
 \frac{\dd s_X}{\dd\ln X}=\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)s_X(1-s_X).
\]
Differentiating \(R'(X)\) with respect to \(X\) gives
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{XR''(X)}{p_X(X)}
 =e_X(1-e_X)+\left(\alpha-\frac1\sigma\right)
 \left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)s_X(1-s_X).
 \label{eq:rewrite-monopoly-proof-curvature}
\end{equation}
At supply levels with \(R'(X)>0\), we have \(0<e_X<1\), so
\(e_X(1-e_X)>0\).
If \((\alpha-1/\sigma)(1-1/\sigma)\geq0\), the remaining term is nonnegative,
so the right-hand side is positive.
If \((\alpha-1/\sigma)(1-1/\sigma)<0\), the parameter restrictions imply
\(0<\alpha<1/\sigma<1\).
Substituting \(e_X=\sigma^{-1}(1-s_X)+\alpha s_X\) and expanding gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 -\frac{XR''(X)}{p_X(X)}
 ={}&\frac1\sigma\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)(1-s_X)^2\\
 &+\left[\frac1{\sigma^2}+2\alpha-\frac{3\alpha}{\sigma}\right]s_X(1-s_X)
 +\alpha(1-\alpha)s_X^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
All three coefficients in this expansion are positive. For the coefficient
of \(s_X(1-s_X)\), this follows from
\[
 \frac1{\sigma^2}+2\alpha-\frac{3\alpha}{\sigma}
 =\left(\frac1\sigma-\alpha\right)^2
 +\alpha\left(2-\frac1\sigma-\alpha\right)>0.
\]
Hence \(R''(X)<0\) at every supply level where marginal revenue
\(R'(X)\) is positive.

As \(X\downarrow0\), the price \(p_X(X)\) tends to infinity, while
\(1-e_X\) converges to a strictly positive value in all three substitution
regimes. Hence \(R'(X)=p_X(X)(1-e_X)\to\infty\). For sufficiently large
\(X\), marginal revenue falls below marginal cost \(1/B\): it converges
to zero from above if \(\sigma\geq1\) and becomes negative if \(\sigma<1\).

Marginal revenue therefore crosses marginal cost at least once. Because
\(R''(X)<0\) wherever \(R'(X)>0\), the crossing is unique and occurs from
above. The unique solution to \(R'(X)=1/B\) therefore maximizes operating
profit globally and satisfies
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc}.

At this solution, \(R''(X)<0\), so the implicit-function theorem applied
to \(R'(X)=1/B\) implies that optimal supply is smooth in \(K,A,L,B\)
for each fixed \(\sigma\).
\end{proof}

The first-order and transversality conditions alone do not establish that
a research trajectory maximizes discounted net profit. The next lemma
provides a sufficient condition using only the research elasticity $\eta$, the
production parameters, and initial AI efficiency relative to its upper
bound. For this verification, the given paths of $K,A,L$ are continuous
and $r$ is locally integrable.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Sufficient conditions for optimal research]
\label{lem:rewrite-developer-verification}
Suppose $\overline B<\infty$ and
\begin{equation}
 \eta\left(1-\frac{B_0}{\overline B}\right)
 \leq\min\left\{\alpha,\frac1\sigma\right\}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-optimality-condition}
\end{equation}
Consider a feasible research trajectory $\{B_t,M_t\}_{t\geq0}$
for problem~\eqref{eq:rewrite-developer-reduced},
with $M_t>0$ at every date and a finite present value of net profit.
If there exists a continuously differentiable shadow-price trajectory
$\{q_t\}_{t\geq0}$ satisfying the research condition
\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc}, the costate equation
\eqref{eq:rewrite-costate}, and the transversality condition
\eqref{eq:rewrite-developer-tvc}, then the research trajectory
maximizes the developer's discounted net profit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification}]
I first bound the response of optimal AI-service supply to efficiency.
I then use this bound and
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-optimality-condition} to make the
research-maximized Hamiltonian concave after a change of state variable.
Concavity bounds the gain from any alternative trajectory by a quantity
that vanishes under the candidate's transversality condition.

\emph{1. Bounding the response of AI-service supply.}
Let $X_t^*(b)$ denote optimal supply at efficiency $b$, and let
$\pi_t(b)$ denote the resulting operating profit in
problem~\eqref{eq:rewrite-service-choice}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice} ensures that this supply is unique
and smooth. Implicit differentiation of the monopoly condition
\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} gives its elasticity with respect to $b$:
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{B,t}(b)\equiv
 \frac{\partial\ln X_t^*(b)}{\partial\ln b}
 =\frac{1-e_X}{e_X(1-e_X)
 +(\alpha-\sigma^{-1})(1-\sigma^{-1})s_X(1-s_X)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-service-capability-elasticity}
\end{equation}
Here $s_X$ and $e_X$ are evaluated at $X_t^*(b)$.
The denominator is positive by
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-proof-curvature}, and $0<e_X<1$ at
the optimum. If $(\alpha-1/\sigma)(1-1/\sigma)\geq0$, the denominator
is at least $e_X(1-e_X)$, with
$e_X\geq\min\{\alpha,1/\sigma\}$.
In the remaining case, $\alpha<1/\sigma<1$. Substituting
$e_X=\sigma^{-1}(1-s_X)+\alpha s_X$ and subtracting
$\alpha(1-e_X)$ from the denominator gives
\[
 (\sigma^{-1}-\alpha)(1-s_X)
 \left[(1-\sigma^{-1})(1-s_X)+(\sigma^{-1}-\alpha)s_X\right]\geq0.
\]
Thus, in both cases, the denominator is at least
$\min\{\alpha,1/\sigma\}(1-e_X)$, so
\begin{equation}
 0<\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)\leq\max\{\sigma,1/\alpha\}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-service-elasticity-bound}
\end{equation}
This bound holds at every date and every positive efficiency, independently
of the quantities of capital and effective labor.

\emph{2. Transforming AI efficiency and maximizing over research.}
Every feasible trajectory remains below $\overline B$ at finite dates.
Indeed, feasibility requires nonnegative, locally integrable research
expenditure and a locally absolutely continuous state satisfying the
RSI technology~\eqref{eq:rewrite-capability-law} almost everywhere.
Integrating that law gives
\begin{equation}
 \overline B-B_t=(\overline B-B_0)
 \exp\left\{-\frac{\chi}{\overline B}
 \int_0^t(B_sM_s)^\eta\dd s\right\}>0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-cap-gap}
\end{equation}
Continuity of $B$, local integrability of $M$, and $0<\eta<1$ make the
exponent finite at every finite date. Hence the relevant efficiency domain,
for both the candidate and every feasible alternative, is $[B_0,\overline B)$.

I express the research problem in terms of an increasing transformation
$S(B)$ of AI efficiency. The transformation is chosen so that
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-optimality-condition} ensures
concavity of the maximized Hamiltonian, as verified in Step 3. Define
\begin{equation}
 v=\frac{1-\eta}{\eta\psi(B_0)},\qquad
 p=\frac1{\eta\psi(B_0)}-1,\qquad
 S(B)=\int_0^B\frac{z^{v-1}}{\psi(z)}\,\dd z.
 \label{eq:rewrite-optimality-state}
\end{equation}
Both exponents are positive. The transformation maps
$[B_0,\overline B)$ increasingly onto $[S(B_0),\infty)$ and has
inverse $B(S)$, with derivative $B'(S)=\psi(B)B^{1-v}$.

Writing $S_t=S(B_t)$, the chain rule applied to the RSI technology gives
\[
 \dot S_t=\chi B_t^{v-1+\eta}M_t^\eta.
\]
The shadow price in the new coordinates is
$q_{S,t}=q_tB'(S_t)=q_t\psi(B_t)B_t^{1-v}>0$.

At each date, hold this shadow price at its candidate value and maximize
the transformed Hamiltonian over research expenditure:
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_t(S)=
 \max_{\widetilde M\geq0}
 \left\{\pi_t(B(S))-\widetilde M+
 q_{S,t}\chi B(S)^{v-1+\eta}\widetilde M^\eta\right\}.
\]
The expression is strictly concave in $\widetilde M$. Its first-order
condition, together with the candidate's research
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc}, gives
$\widetilde M=M_t[B(S)/B_t]^p$, since
$p=(v-1+\eta)/(1-\eta)$. Substituting this choice yields
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_t(S)
 =\pi_t(B(S))+\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}M_t
 \left[\frac{B(S)}{B_t}\right]^p.
 \label{eq:rewrite-optimality-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}

\emph{3. Establishing concavity.}
Fix a date and write $b=B(S)$. I show that each term in
\eqref{eq:rewrite-optimality-hamiltonian} is concave in $S$ over the
entire feasible efficiency domain.
The envelope condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-envelope} gives
$\pi_t'(b)=X_t^*(b)/b^2$. Differentiating this expression gives
$\pi_t''(b)=X_t^*(b)[\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)-2]/b^3$.
Using $B'(S)=\psi(b)b^{1-v}$ and $\psi'(b)=-1/\overline B$,
the chain rule therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\dd^2\pi_t(B(S))}{\dd S^2}
 =X_t^*(b)b^{-1-2v}\psi(b)
 \left[(\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)-1-v)\psi(b)-\frac b{\overline B}\right].
\]
Applying the same chain rule to the power $B(S)^p$ in the research term gives
\[
 \frac{\dd^2 B(S)^p}{\dd S^2}
 =p\,b^{p-2v}\psi(b)
 \left[(p-v)\psi(b)-\frac b{\overline B}\right].
\]
The parameter condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-optimality-condition}
and the supply-elasticity bound~\eqref{eq:rewrite-service-elasticity-bound}
imply
\[
 \varepsilon_{B,t}(b)\leq\frac1{\eta\psi(B_0)}=p+1.
\]
The bracket in the operating-profit derivative is therefore no greater
than the bracket in the research-term derivative. Substitution of $p$,
$v$, and $\psi(b)=1-b/\overline B$ reduces the latter bracket to
\[
 (p-v)\psi(b)-\frac b{\overline B}
 =\frac{B_0-b}{\overline B-B_0}\leq0.
\]
Both second derivatives are nonpositive for every $b\geq B_0$.
Consequently, the maximized Hamiltonian is concave in $S$ and lies
below its tangent at the candidate state $S_t$.

\emph{4. Comparing discounted net profits.}
Let $D_t=\exp(-\int_0^t r_s\dd s)$ and $\mu_t=D_tq_{S,t}$.
Differentiating~\eqref{eq:rewrite-optimality-hamiltonian} at $S_t$ and
using the envelope condition gives
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(S_t)
 =\psi(B_t)B_t^{-v}
 \left[U_t+\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}pM_t\right].
\]
The research condition and the RSI law imply
$q_t\eta\dot B_t=M_t$. Differentiating
$q_{S,t}=q_t\psi(B_t)B_t^{1-v}$ and using this identity and the
costate equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-costate} gives
\[
 \dot q_{S,t}=r_tq_{S,t}-\widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(S_t),
 \qquad
 \dot\mu_t=-D_t\widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(S_t).
\]

Consider any feasible alternative
$\{\widetilde B_t,\widetilde M_t\}_{t\geq0}$ starting from the same $B_0$,
and write $\widetilde S_t=S(\widetilde B_t)$.
At the candidate shadow price, the alternative's Hamiltonian is at most
$\widehat{\mathcal H}_t(\widetilde S_t)$, whereas the candidate attains
$\widehat{\mathcal H}_t(S_t)$.
The Hamiltonian equals current net profit plus $q_{S,t}\dot S_t$.
Subtracting these state-accumulation terms and applying concavity
therefore bounds the difference in discounted net profit, almost everywhere:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &D_t\left[\pi_t(\widetilde B_t)-\widetilde M_t-\pi_t(B_t)+M_t\right]\\
 &\quad\leq
 D_t\widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(S_t)(\widetilde S_t-S_t)
 -\mu_t(\dot{\widetilde S}_t-\dot S_t)\\
 &\quad=-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}
 \left[\mu_t(\widetilde S_t-S_t)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses the transformed costate equation.

Let $J_T$ and $\widetilde J_T$ denote the candidate's and the alternative's
discounted net profits through date $T$. Integrating and using their
common initial state gives
\[
 \widetilde J_T-J_T
 \leq-\mu_T(\widetilde S_T-S_T)\leq\mu_TS_T,
\]
because $\mu_T>0$ and $\widetilde S_T\geq0$.
Thus any gain from the alternative is bounded above by the candidate's
terminal value $\mu_TS_T$.

It remains to show that this terminal value vanishes.
Since $\psi(z)\geq\psi(B_t)$ for $0\leq z\leq B_t$, the definition of $S$
gives
\[
 0\leq q_{S,t}S_t
 =q_t\psi(B_t)B_t^{1-v}
 \int_0^{B_t}\frac{z^{v-1}}{\psi(z)}\,\dd z
 \leq\frac{q_tB_t}{v}.
\]
The candidate's transversality condition therefore implies
$\mu_TS_T\leq D_Tq_TB_T/v\to0$.
Because its present value of net profit is finite,
\[
 \limsup_{T\to\infty}\widetilde J_T
 \leq\lim_{T\to\infty}J_T<\infty.
\]
No feasible alternative yields a higher present value of net profit.
\end{proof}

Condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-optimality-condition} is sufficient,
not necessary. For any fixed production parameters and research elasticity $\eta$,
it holds when $B_0$ is sufficiently close to $\overline B$.
The lemma verifies optimality of a candidate trajectory; it does not
establish that such a trajectory exists.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-monopoly-threshold}]
\phantomsection
\label{proof:rewrite-monopoly-threshold}
Fix $\sigma>1$. The limiting returns in
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-terminal-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-marginal-cost-return}, derived above, satisfy
\[
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}(B)=\mathcal R\bigl(B/(1-\alpha)\bigr).
\]
Both returns increase strictly with $B$. Since each threshold is the
efficiency at which the corresponding return equals $\rho+\gamma$,
\[
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}\bigl((1-\alpha)\mathcal B(\sigma)\bigr)
 =\mathcal R\bigl(\mathcal B(\sigma)\bigr)=\rho+\gamma.
\]
Uniqueness of the competitive efficiency threshold gives
$\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)=(1-\alpha)\mathcal B(\sigma)
<\mathcal B(\sigma)$, establishing
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-marginal-cost-threshold}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:rewrite-monopoly-bottleneck}]
\phantomsection
\label{proof:rewrite-monopoly-bottleneck}
Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-monopoly-threshold} ensures that the
interval in equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-competition-escapes-bottleneck}
is nonempty. Fix $\overline B$ in this interval. The labor-bottleneck
construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-capped-substitutes-labor-existence}
uses initial values satisfying
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-labor-local-initial}, with
$B_0=\overline B-d_0\tau_0$, $d_0$ near $\overline d>0$, and
$\tau_0=1/(A_0N_0)>0$ arbitrarily small.
Since $\overline B>\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)$, I can restrict this
construction to $B_0>\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)$ while preserving
a nonempty open set of initial states.

From each state in this set, the constructed monopoly equilibrium has
$B\to\overline B$ and $g_{Y/N},g_w\to\gamma$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-competitive-equilibrium} supplies a
competitive equilibrium from the same state. Since
$B_0>\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)$, this equilibrium is AI-dominated,
with $B=B_0$ and both limiting growth rates strictly above $\gamma$.
This proves the comparison without requiring monopoly equilibrium
existence from arbitrary initial states or global uniqueness under monopoly.

The accompanying comparison of AI services follows from the CES share
identity in equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-frontier-static-shares} and $L=N$.
Under monopoly, $s_X$ converges to an interior value, so $X/(AL)$
converges to a positive constant. Under competition, $s_X\to1$;
since $\sigma>1$, the same identity gives $X/(AL)\to\infty$.
The common paths of $A$ and $L$ therefore imply that AI services
eventually exceed their monopoly level under competition, without
establishing their ordering at every date.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:rewrite-competitive-monopoly-growth}]
\phantomsection
\label{proof:rewrite-competitive-monopoly-growth}
Equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-terminal-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-marginal-cost-return} imply
\[
 \mathcal R(\overline B)
 =\mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}\bigl((1-\alpha)\overline B\bigr).
\]
Since $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}$ is strictly increasing in AI efficiency,
it follows that
\[
 \mathcal R(\overline B)\gtrless\mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}(B_0)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (1-\alpha)\overline B\gtrless B_0,
\]
with equality on one side if and only if equality holds on the other.
Under the two AI-dominated equilibria, these returns are the limiting
interest rates. Equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-substitutes-ai-limits},
\eqref{eq:rewrite-substitutes-wage-growth}, and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-competitive-ai-growth} express limiting
output-per-worker and wage growth as strictly increasing functions of
the limiting interest rate, with slopes one and $1/\sigma$, respectively.
Since the parameters are common to both economies, the same ordering
applies to both growth rates, including the equality case.
\end{proof}
