\subsection{Developer optimization}
\label{app:rewrite-developer-proofs}

The research problem uses operating profit after AI-service supply has
been chosen optimally. I first establish that
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} identifies the unique
profit-maximizing supply, so I can use this condition when constructing
equilibrium trajectories.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Optimal AI-service supply]
\label{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice}
The developer's operating profit in
problem~\eqref{eq:rewrite-service-choice} has a unique global maximizer
$X>0$. This optimal supply of AI services is characterized by
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} and varies smoothly with
$K,A,L,$ and $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice}]
Hold \(K,A,L,B\) fixed and let \(R(X)=p_X(X)X\) denote AI-service revenue.
I show that marginal revenue crosses the constant marginal cost \(1/B\)
exactly once, from above, so operating profit has a unique global maximum.
The inverse-demand elasticity in \eqref{eq:rewrite-inverse-demand-elasticity}
and differentiation of \eqref{eq:rewrite-sx} give
\[
 R'(X)=p_X(X)(1-e_X),\qquad
 \frac{\dd s_X}{\dd\ln X}=\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)s_X(1-s_X).
\]
Differentiating \(R'(X)\) with respect to \(X\) gives
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{XR''(X)}{p_X(X)}
 =e_X(1-e_X)+\left(\alpha-\frac1\sigma\right)
 \left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)s_X(1-s_X).
 \label{eq:rewrite-monopoly-proof-curvature}
\end{equation}
At supply levels with \(R'(X)>0\), we have \(0<e_X<1\), so
\(e_X(1-e_X)>0\).
If \((\alpha-1/\sigma)(1-1/\sigma)\geq0\), the remaining term is nonnegative,
so the right-hand side is positive.
If \((\alpha-1/\sigma)(1-1/\sigma)<0\), the parameter restrictions imply
\(0<\alpha<1/\sigma<1\), and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 -\frac{XR''(X)}{p_X(X)}
 ={}&\frac1\sigma\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)(1-s_X)^2\\
 &+\left[\frac1{\sigma^2}+2\alpha-\frac{3\alpha}{\sigma}\right]s_X(1-s_X)
 +\alpha(1-\alpha)s_X^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
All three coefficients in this expansion are positive. For the coefficient
of \(s_X(1-s_X)\), this follows from
\[
 \frac1{\sigma^2}+2\alpha-\frac{3\alpha}{\sigma}
 =\left(\frac1\sigma-\alpha\right)^2
 +\alpha\left(2-\frac1\sigma-\alpha\right)>0.
\]
Hence \(R''(X)<0\) at every supply level where marginal revenue
\(R'(X)\) is positive.

As \(X\downarrow0\), revenue is asymptotically proportional to \(X^{1-1/\sigma}\) when
\(\sigma>1\), to \(X^{(1-\alpha)\omega_X}\) when \(\sigma=1\), and to
\(X^{1-\alpha}\) when \(\sigma<1\). Every exponent lies between zero and
one, and \(R'(X)=(1-e_X)R(X)/X\) gives \(R'(X)\to\infty\).
As \(X\to\infty\), marginal revenue converges to
zero from above if \(\sigma\geq1\); if \(\sigma<1\), marginal revenue
becomes negative at sufficiently large \(X\).
Together with \(R''(X)<0\) wherever \(R'(X)>0\), these limits imply that
the derivative of operating profit, \(R'(X)-1/B\), changes sign exactly
once, from positive to negative. The unique solution to \(R'(X)=1/B\)
therefore maximizes operating profit globally and satisfies
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc}.

At this solution, \(R''(X)<0\), so the implicit-function theorem applied
to \(R'(X)=1/B\) implies that optimal supply is smooth in \(K,A,L,B\)
for each fixed \(\sigma\).
\end{proof}

The first-order and transversality conditions alone do not establish that
a research trajectory maximizes discounted net profit. The next lemma
provides a global optimality test by adding the following inequality for
the candidate $\{B_t,M_t\}_{t\geq0}$ and alternative AI-efficiency levels $b$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \pi_t(b)-\pi_t(B_t)
 &+\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}M_t
 \left[
 \left(\frac{b}{B_t}\right)^{\eta/(1-\eta)}-1
 \right]\\
 &\leq
 \frac{\overline B-B_t}{B_t}(U_t+M_t)
 \log\!\left(
 \frac{\overline B-B_t}{\overline B-b}
 \right),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support}
\end{equation}
Here $\pi_t(b)$ is optimized operating profit at efficiency $b$, as defined
in equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-service-choice}, and $U_t$ is the inference
compute used at the candidate efficiency $B_t$ after optimizing AI-service
supply.

\begin{lemma}[Sufficient conditions for optimal research]
\label{lem:rewrite-developer-verification}
Suppose $\overline B<\infty$. Consider a feasible research trajectory
$\{B_t,M_t\}_{t\geq0}$ for problem~\eqref{eq:rewrite-developer-reduced},
with $M_t>0$ at every date and a finite present value of net profit.
Suppose there exists a continuously differentiable shadow-price trajectory
$\{q_t\}_{t\geq0}$ satisfying the research condition
\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc}, the costate equation
\eqref{eq:rewrite-costate}, and the transversality condition
\eqref{eq:rewrite-developer-tvc}.
If inequality~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support} also holds for every
$t\geq0$ and $b\in[B_0,\overline B)$, then the research trajectory maximizes
the developer's discounted net profit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification}]
I compare the candidate with any feasible research trajectory starting
from the same $B_0$. The key step is to bound the alternative's gain in
discounted net profit by a terminal term that vanishes under the
candidate's transversality condition.

\emph{1. Transforming AI efficiency.}
I use a change of variable to remove $\psi(B)$ from the RSI technology.
For this transformation to apply to every feasible trajectory, AI
efficiency must remain below $\overline B$ at every finite date.
Feasibility requires nonnegative, locally integrable research expenditure
and a locally absolutely continuous state satisfying
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-capability-law} almost everywhere. Integrating
the RSI technology gives
\begin{equation}
 \overline B-B_t=(\overline B-B_0)
 \exp\left\{-\frac{\chi}{\overline B}
 \int_0^t(B_sM_s)^\eta\dd s\right\}>0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-cap-gap}
\end{equation}
Continuity of $B$, local integrability of $M$, and $0<\eta<1$ make the
exponent finite at every finite date. Thus every feasible trajectory
remains in $[B_0,\overline B)$.

Define the transformed efficiency variable and its inverse by
\begin{equation}
 R_B(B)=-\overline B\log\left(1-\frac{B}{\overline B}\right),
 \qquad
 B(R_B)=\overline B\left(1-e^{-R_B/\overline B}\right).
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-depth}
\end{equation}
The transformation $R_B(B)$ maps $B\in[B_0,\overline B)$ into
$R_B\in[R_{B,0},\infty)$, where $R_{B,t}=R_B(B_t)$ and
$R_{B,0}=R_B(B_0)$. Since $R_B'(B)=1/\psi(B)$, this transformation removes
the factor $\psi(B)$ from the RSI technology~\eqref{eq:rewrite-capability-law}:
\begin{equation}
 \dot R_B=\chi[B(R_B)M]^\eta.
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-depth-law}
\end{equation}
The corresponding current-value shadow price is $q_{R,t}=q_t\psi(B_t)$,
which is positive by the research condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc}.

\emph{2. Comparing Hamiltonians.}
To compare the candidate with alternative trajectories, I maximize the
Hamiltonian over research expenditure at each alternative transformed
efficiency $\widetilde R_B$. Throughout this comparison, the shadow price
$q_{R,t}$ is held at its value along the candidate trajectory:
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_t(\widetilde R_B)
 \equiv\max_{\widetilde M\geq0}
 \left\{\pi_t(B(\widetilde R_B))-\widetilde M
 +q_{R,t}\chi[B(\widetilde R_B)\widetilde M]^\eta\right\}.
\]
Because $0<\eta<1$, the expression being maximized is strictly concave in
research expenditure. Using the candidate's research
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc}, the maximizing expenditure at
$\widetilde R_B$ is
\[
 M_t\left[\frac{B(\widetilde R_B)}{B_t}\right]^{\eta/(1-\eta)},
\]
where $M_t$ is the candidate's research expenditure. Substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_t(\widetilde R_B)
 =\pi_t(B(\widetilde R_B))+
 \frac{1-\eta}{\eta}M_t
 \left[\frac{B(\widetilde R_B)}{B_t}\right]^{\eta/(1-\eta)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-maximized-depth-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Differentiating the maximized Hamiltonian with respect to transformed
efficiency $R_B$
and using the envelope condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-envelope} gives
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(R_{B,t})
 =\frac{\psi(B_t)}{B_t}(U_t+M_t).
 \label{eq:rewrite-depth-hamiltonian-slope}
\end{equation}
Because
\[
 R_B(b)-R_{B,t}
 =\overline B\log\!\left(\frac{\overline B-B_t}{\overline B-b}\right),
\]
setting $b=B(\widetilde R_B)$ makes
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support} equivalent to
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal H}_t(\widetilde R_B)
 \leq\widehat{\mathcal H}_t(R_{B,t})
 +\widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(R_{B,t})
 (\widetilde R_B-R_{B,t}).
\]
This inequality holds for every $\widetilde R_B\geq R_{B,0}$: the
maximized Hamiltonian lies below its tangent at the candidate state.

\emph{3. Comparing discounted net profits.}
I now combine the Hamiltonian inequality with the costate equation and
the transversality condition.
Let $D_t=\exp(-\int_0^t r_s\dd s)$ be the discount factor and
$\mu_t=D_tq_{R,t}$ the present-value shadow price of the transformed state $R_B$.
The research condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc} and the RSI
technology~\eqref{eq:rewrite-capability-law} imply $q_t\eta\dot B_t=M_t$.
Differentiating $q_{R,t}=q_t\psi(B_t)$ and using the costate
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-costate} therefore gives
\[
 \dot q_{R,t}=r_tq_{R,t}-\frac{\psi(B_t)}{B_t}(U_t+M_t),
 \qquad
 \dot\mu_t=-D_t\widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(R_{B,t}).
\]

Consider any feasible alternative $\{\widetilde B_t,\widetilde M_t\}_{t\geq0}$
starting from the same $B_0$, and write $\widetilde R_{B,t}=R_B(\widetilde B_t)$.
Let
\[
 J_T=\int_0^T D_t[\pi_t(B_t)-M_t]\dd t
\]
denote discounted net profit through date $T$ under the candidate, and let
$\widetilde J_T$ denote the corresponding value under the alternative.
Evaluated at the candidate's shadow price $q_{R,t}$, the alternative's
Hamiltonian cannot exceed
$\widehat{\mathcal H}_t(\widetilde R_{B,t})$, whereas the candidate attains
$\widehat{\mathcal H}_t(R_{B,t})$. Applying
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support} and the transformed costate
equation yields, for almost every $t$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &D_t\left[
 \pi_t(\widetilde B_t)-\widetilde M_t
 -\pi_t(B_t)+M_t
 \right]\\
 &\quad\leq D_t\widehat{\mathcal H}'_t(R_{B,t})
 (\widetilde R_{B,t}-R_{B,t})
 -\mu_t(\dot{\widetilde R}_{B,t}-\dot R_{B,t})\\
 &\quad=-\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}
 \left[\mu_t(\widetilde R_{B,t}-R_{B,t})\right].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Integrating from zero to $T$ and using the common initial
state gives
\[
 \widetilde J_T-J_T
 \leq-\mu_T(\widetilde R_{B,T}-R_{B,T})
 \leq\mu_TR_{B,T},
\]
where the second inequality uses $\mu_T>0$ and $\widetilde R_{B,T}\geq0$.
To show that this upper bound vanishes, I relate the transformed state
to the original state used in the developer's transversality condition:
\[
 \psi(B_t)R_{B,t}
 =-(\overline B-B_t)\log\left(1-\frac{B_t}{\overline B}\right)
 \leq B_t.
\]
The transversality condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-developer-tvc} therefore gives
\[
 0\leq\mu_TR_{B,T}
 =D_Tq_T\psi(B_T)R_{B,T}
 \leq D_Tq_TB_T\longrightarrow0.
\]
The candidate's present value of net profit is finite by assumption. Hence
\[
 \limsup_{T\to\infty}\widetilde J_T
 \leq\lim_{T\to\infty}J_T<\infty,
\]
so no feasible alternative yields a higher present value of net profit.
Concavity of $\widehat{\mathcal H}_t$ in $R_B$ is sufficient for
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support}, but is not required.
\end{proof}

The next lemma gives conditions for concavity that ensure
inequality~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support} holds. I use these
conditions to apply Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification} when
verifying developer optimality in the equilibrium existence proof and
the numerical checks.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Sufficient conditions for Hamiltonian concavity]
\label{lem:rewrite-hamiltonian-concavity}
Suppose $\overline B<\infty$.
Consider a feasible research trajectory $\{B_t,M_t\}_{t\geq0}$ with
$M_t>0$ at every date, satisfying the research
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-foc}.
Let $X_t^*(b)$ denote the optimal AI-service choice in
problem~\eqref{eq:rewrite-service-choice} at efficiency $b$, and let
$\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)=\partial\ln X_t^*(b)/\partial\ln b$
denote its elasticity with respect to AI efficiency.
The maximized Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:rewrite-maximized-depth-hamiltonian}
is concave in $R_B$ on $[R_B(B_0),\infty)$ at every date if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.5em]
\item For operating profit, the following inequality holds for every
$t\geq0$ and $b\in[B_0,\overline B)$:
\begin{equation}
 \psi(b)[\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)-2]\leq\frac{b}{\overline B},
 \label{eq:rewrite-capped-concavity-gate}
\end{equation}
\item For the optimized research term, the following inequality holds:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{B_0}{\overline B}\geq
 \max\left\{0,\frac{2\eta-1}{\eta}\right\}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-depth-research-concavity}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
These conditions therefore imply
inequality~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support} in
Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-hamiltonian-concavity}]
Fix a date $t$ and write $b=B(R_B)$. In
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-maximized-depth-hamiltonian}, the candidate
values $B_t$ and $M_t$ are held fixed. I show that each of the two terms
in that Hamiltonian is concave in $R_B$ under the corresponding condition.

\emph{1. Operating profit.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice} ensures that $X_t^*(b)$ is unique
and smooth. Let $s_{X,t}(b)$ and $e_{X,t}(b)$ denote the elasticities in
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-sx} and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-inverse-demand-elasticity},
evaluated at this optimal supply. Implicit differentiation of the monopoly
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} gives
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{B,t}(b)\equiv
 \frac{\partial\ln X_t^*(b)}{\partial\ln b}
 =\frac{1-e_{X,t}(b)}{e_{X,t}(b)[1-e_{X,t}(b)]
 +(\alpha-\sigma^{-1})(1-\sigma^{-1})
 s_{X,t}(b)[1-s_{X,t}(b)]}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-service-capability-elasticity}
\end{equation}
The denominator is strictly positive by
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-proof-curvature}. The envelope
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-envelope} gives
$\pi_t'(b)=X_t^*(b)/b^2$, so
\begin{equation}
 \pi_t''(b)=\frac{X_t^*(b)}{b^3}[\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)-2].
 \label{eq:rewrite-profit-curvature}
\end{equation}

The transformation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-depth} satisfies
$B'(R_B)=\psi(b)$ and $B''(R_B)=-\psi(b)/\overline B$.
The chain rule therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathrm d^2\pi_t(B(R_B))}{\mathrm dR_B^2}
 =\frac{X_t^*(b)\psi(b)}{b^3}
 \left[\psi(b)(\varepsilon_{B,t}(b)-2)
 -\frac{b}{\overline B}\right].
 \label{eq:rewrite-capped-profit-curvature}
\end{equation}
Condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-capped-concavity-gate} makes this second
derivative nonpositive for every $b\in[B_0,\overline B)$. Hence operating
profit $\pi_t(B(R_B))$ is concave throughout $R_B\in[R_B(B_0),\infty)$.

\emph{2. The optimized research term.}
The second term in \eqref{eq:rewrite-maximized-depth-hamiltonian} is a
positive multiple of $B(R_B)^{\eta/(1-\eta)}$. The second derivative of
this power with respect to $R_B$ is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathrm d^2 B(R_B)^{\eta/(1-\eta)}}{\mathrm dR_B^2}
 =\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}b^{\eta/(1-\eta)-2}\psi(b)
 \left[\frac{2\eta-1}{1-\eta}\psi(b)-\frac b{\overline B}\right].
 \label{eq:rewrite-depth-research-curvature}
\end{equation}
The expression in brackets is nonpositive exactly when
$b/\overline B\geq(2\eta-1)/\eta$. Because $b\geq B_0$,
condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-depth-research-concavity} ensures that the
research term is concave throughout the same domain.

Both terms are therefore concave in $R_B$. Their sum, the maximized
Hamiltonian, lies below its tangent at the candidate state. As shown in
the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification}, this is
inequality~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support}.
\end{proof}

Condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-depth-research-concavity} holds automatically
if $\eta\leq1/2$. For any fixed $0<\eta<1$, that condition also holds when
$B_0$ is sufficiently close to $\overline B$. The two conditions in
Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-hamiltonian-concavity} are sufficient, not necessary,
for inequality~\eqref{eq:rewrite-hamiltonian-support}: the inequality can
hold even when one of the two conditions fails.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-monopoly-threshold}]
\phantomsection
\label{proof:rewrite-monopoly-threshold}
Fix $\sigma>1$. The limiting returns in
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-terminal-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-marginal-cost-return}, derived above, satisfy
\[
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}(B)=\mathcal R\bigl(B/(1-\alpha)\bigr).
\]
Both returns increase strictly with $B$. Since each threshold is the
efficiency at which the corresponding return equals $\rho+\gamma$,
\[
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}\bigl((1-\alpha)\mathcal B(\sigma)\bigr)
 =\mathcal R\bigl(\mathcal B(\sigma)\bigr)=\rho+\gamma.
\]
Uniqueness of the competitive efficiency threshold gives
$\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)=(1-\alpha)\mathcal B(\sigma)
<\mathcal B(\sigma)$, establishing
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-marginal-cost-threshold}.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:rewrite-monopoly-bottleneck}]
\phantomsection
\label{proof:rewrite-monopoly-bottleneck}
Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-monopoly-threshold} ensures that the
interval in equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-competition-escapes-bottleneck}
is nonempty. Fix $\overline B$ in this interval. The labor-bottleneck
construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-capped-substitutes-labor-existence}
uses initial values satisfying
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-labor-local-initial}, with
$B_0=\overline B-d_0\tau_0$, $d_0$ near $\overline d>0$, and
$\tau_0=1/(A_0N_0)>0$ arbitrarily small.
Since $\overline B>\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)$, I can restrict this
construction to $B_0>\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)$ while preserving
a nonempty open set of initial states.

From each state in this set, the constructed monopoly equilibrium has
$B\to\overline B$ and $g_{Y/N},g_w\to\gamma$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-competitive-equilibrium} supplies a
competitive equilibrium from the same state. Since
$B_0>\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(\sigma)$, this equilibrium is AI-dominated,
with $B=B_0$ and both limiting growth rates strictly above $\gamma$.
This proves the comparison without requiring monopoly equilibrium
existence from arbitrary initial states or global uniqueness under monopoly.

The accompanying comparison of AI services follows from the CES share
identity in equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-frontier-static-shares} and $L=N$.
Under monopoly, $s_X$ converges to an interior value, so $X/(AL)$
converges to a positive constant. Under competition, $s_X\to1$;
since $\sigma>1$, the same identity gives $X/(AL)\to\infty$.
The common paths of $A$ and $L$ therefore imply that AI services
eventually exceed their monopoly level under competition, without
establishing their ordering at every date.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:rewrite-competitive-monopoly-growth}]
\phantomsection
\label{proof:rewrite-competitive-monopoly-growth}
Equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-terminal-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-marginal-cost-return} imply
\[
 \mathcal R(\overline B)
 =\mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}\bigl((1-\alpha)\overline B\bigr).
\]
Since $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}$ is strictly increasing in AI efficiency,
it follows that
\[
 \mathcal R(\overline B)\gtrless\mathcal R^{\mathrm{mc}}(B_0)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (1-\alpha)\overline B\gtrless B_0,
\]
with equality on one side if and only if equality holds on the other.
Under the two AI-dominated equilibria, these returns are the limiting
interest rates. Equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-substitutes-ai-limits},
\eqref{eq:rewrite-substitutes-wage-growth}, and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-competitive-ai-growth} express limiting
output-per-worker and wage growth as strictly increasing functions of
the limiting interest rate, with slopes one and $1/\sigma$, respectively.
Since the parameters are common to both economies, the same ordering
applies to both growth rates, including the equality case.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-research-scale}]
\label{proof:rewrite-research-scale}
I construct a sequence of research trajectories whose discounted net profit
grows without bound, although each trajectory has finite expenditure and
AI efficiency. Write $\theta=(1-\alpha)/\alpha$. For a research trajectory
$\{M_t\}_{t\in[0,T]}$, discounted net profit on the fixed horizon is
\[
 J_T(M)=\int_0^T
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 [\pi(B_t)-M_t]\dd t.
\]
Integrability of $r$ bounds the discount factor above and away from zero
on $[0,T]$.
At the optimal service choice, equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-price}
and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} imply
\[
 \pi(B)=p_XX-\frac XB
 =e_Xp_XX=(1-\alpha)e_Xs_XY.
\]
For $\sigma>1$, marginal revenue is positive at every finite $X$
and approaches zero as $X\to\infty$, as established in
Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-monopoly-choice}. The monopoly condition
therefore implies $X\to\infty$ as $B\to\infty$, holding $K,A,L$
fixed. Consequently, $s_X\to1$ and $e_X\to\alpha$.
Substituting these limits into
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-substitutes-dated-output-capital} gives
\[
 \pi(B)\sim\kappa_XKB^\theta,\qquad
 \kappa_X=\alpha(1-\alpha)
 \left[(1-\alpha)^2\omega_X^{\sigma/(\sigma-1)}\right]^\theta>0.
\]
The operating-profit asymptotic is uniform over dates in $[0,T]$ because
$K,A,L$ are continuous, bounded, and bounded away from zero
on $[0,T]$. Thus a common lower bound proportional to $B^\theta$ applies
at every date once $B$ is sufficiently large.

The research trajectories constructed below are deviations in the
developer's optimization problem, not alternative equilibrium allocations.
Fix $\Delta\in(0,T)$, choose $M=\mathcal M$ on $[0,\Delta]$, and choose
$M=0$ afterward. The uncapped RSI technology gives
\[
 B_{\mathcal M}(t)^{1-\eta}=B_0^{1-\eta}
 +(1-\eta)\chi\mathcal M^\eta\min\{t,\Delta\}.
\]
As $\mathcal M\to\infty$, efficiency after the research burst is
asymptotically proportional to
$\mathcal M^{\eta/(1-\eta)}$. Discounted operating profit over the remaining
interval therefore grows in proportion to
$\mathcal M^{\eta\theta/(1-\eta)}$, while research expenditure grows only
linearly in $\mathcal M$.
Since optimized operating profit is nonnegative, a lower bound on discounted
net profit counts only operating profits earned after research stops, while
subtracting all research expenditure. For all sufficiently large $\mathcal M$,
\begin{equation}
 J_T(\mathcal M)\geq c_\pi\mathcal M^{
 \eta(1-\alpha)/[\alpha(1-\eta)]}
 -c_M\mathcal M,
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-burst-payoff}
\end{equation}
where $c_\pi,c_M>0$ are independent of $\mathcal M$.
The exponent exceeds one because $\eta>\alpha$,
so $J_T(\mathcal M)\to+\infty$ as $\mathcal M\to\infty$.
Each finite $\mathcal M$ keeps AI efficiency and discounted net profit
finite on $[0,T]$; it is the sequence of payoffs that is unbounded.

Now consider an infinite-horizon equilibrium candidate
$\{\widehat B_t,\widehat M_t\}$ with finite discounted net profit
$\widehat V_0$, and let $\widehat J_T$ denote its payoff through $T$.
After $T$, continue the deviation using $\widehat M_t$.
For sufficiently large $\mathcal M$, the deviation has
$B_{\mathcal M}(T)>\widehat B_T$. Because
$\mathrm d(B^{1-\eta})/\mathrm dt=(1-\eta)\chi M^\eta$,
this efficiency advantage persists under the common subsequent expenditure.
Operating profit is increasing in efficiency by
equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-envelope}, so the continuation payoff is
at least $\widehat V_0-\widehat J_T$. The full deviation therefore yields
\[
 J_\infty(\mathcal M)\geq J_T(\mathcal M)
 +\widehat V_0-\widehat J_T\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
No candidate with finite discounted net profit can be optimal.
Moreover, every equilibrium must have finite discounted developer profit.
Indeed, integrating the household budget~\eqref{eq:rewrite-household-budget}
and using its Euler equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-euler}
and transversality condition~\eqref{eq:rewrite-household-tvc} gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty
 e^{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s}(w_tN_t+\Pi_t)\dd t
 =\frac{C_0}{\rho-n}-K_0.
\]
Since labor income is nonnegative, discounted developer profit is
either finite or minus infinity. A payoff of minus infinity cannot be
optimal because
stopping research yields nonnegative operating profit.
Hence no equilibrium exists under the stated restrictions.
This argument does not establish a finite-time singularity.
\end{proof}
