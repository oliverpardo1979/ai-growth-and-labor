\subsection{From competition to monopoly}
\label{subsec:rewrite-competitive-transition}

I now ask whether research financed by monopoly rents can compensate for
the restriction on AI use caused by monopoly pricing and allow an economy
to escape the labor bottleneck. I set $\sigma=1.5$ and study
$\chi=7.5$ and $\chi=1.5$. Unlike the preceding exercises, RSI is available
before date zero, with the same positive $\chi$ as after the change.
Research is nevertheless zero under the competitive arrangement in
Section~\ref{sec:rewrite-competition}, because improvements cannot be
privately appropriated.

I retain the technology parameters in Table~\ref{tab:rewrite-rsi-parameters},
but choose initial capital to place the economy on its competitive
balanced-growth path at the same $B_0$.
%, but choose $K_0=6.535$ to place the economy on its competitive balanced growth path and at the same $B_0$ as before
Before the change, $p_X=1/B_0$, $r=\rho+\gamma$, and
$K_0/Y_{0^-}=3.3$. Initial efficiency lies below
$\mathcal B^{\mathrm{mc}}(1.5)$, so continued competition preserves the
labor-bottleneck. The upper bound instead exceeds $\mathcal B(1.5)$,
allowing the monopoly paths considered here to approach AI-dominated growth.

At date zero, an unexpected permanent exclusive right covers both the
existing AI technology and its subsequent improvements. In particular,
competitive suppliers can no longer use the old technology. This experiment changes research incentives and pricing,
not the RSI technology. It abstracts from the cost of enforcing exclusivity
and is not an optimal policy exercise.

The upper row shows the initial restriction in AI use before efficiency
can improve: output falls by $10.4\%$ and wages by $5.4\%$ under either

efficiency enough for output to regain its competitive counterpart around
year 3.8 and wages around year 4.6. With $\chi=1.5$, both remain below
their competitive counterparts throughout the first decade. Consumption per person nevertheless rises on impact, by $8.2\%$ with
$\chi=7.5$ and $3.7\%$ with $\chi=1.5$. %This initial increase in consumption is accompanied by lower physical investment. The initial fall in the return on capital lowers consumption growth relative to continued competition. With $\chi=1.5$, it falls slightly below that counterpart around year 6.1 and regains it around year 11.3.

The growth advantage of monopoly in this exercise is not general.
As Corollary~\ref{cor:rewrite-monopoly-bottleneck} shows, initial AI
efficiency can exceed the competitive threshold while even its upper bound
remains below the monopoly threshold. Competition then sustains
AI-dominated growth, whereas monopoly-led research produces more efficient
AI but leaves the economy labor-bottlenecked. I illustrate this opposite
outcome in the
\href{https://oliverpardo1979.github.io/ai-growth-and-labor/\#subsec:rewrite-monopoly-growth-reversal}{supplementary simulations available in the digital edition}.
